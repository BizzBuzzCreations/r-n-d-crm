## 1. Starting point: what was actually wrong

A full read-through found three problems that made the goal
("detailed audits → personalised email → higher conversion") unreachable as
written, none of them about crawler depth:

1. **Tiering discarded the best prospects.** `tier` was decided by
   `technicalScore` alone (`< 30` → `skip`). A site with the basics right can
   only pass 30 by being badly broken, so an established business actively
   spending on ads with a slow site scored need 25 / opportunity 100 and came
   out `skip`, flagged `technically_sound`. Since `tier` drives the prospect
   table filter and the CSV export, sales was being steered away from exactly
   the prospects worth contacting.

2. **PageSpeed data was missing and its absence read as "fine".** PSI shared
   the 10s page-fetch timeout, so essentially every call aborted. Because the
   failure was swallowed and `psiPerformanceScore` stayed `null`, every
   PSI-dependent scoring branch was skipped — a failed measurement scored
   identically to a perfect one.

3. **No audit finding could reach an email.** `ProspectAudit` and
   `CampaignLead` had no connection whatsoever. The only route between them
   was a CSV export whose importer keeps 7 columns, and the merge tags covered
   names and email only. Tripling crawler depth would not have changed one
   word of any sent email.

## 2. Implemented

### 2.1 Audit data quality and tiering

- PageSpeed got its own `PSI_TIMEOUT_MS` (60s) plus one retry, and the outcome
  is now recorded: `psiStatus` (`pending|ok|failed|skipped`), `psiError`,
  `needsRecrawl`, surfaced in the UI as an `incomplete_audit` flag.
- `runTier1` now checks `res.ok` and records `httpStatus`. A 403/404/bot-wall
  page was previously parsed and scored as the real site, producing confident
  findings about an error page — unacceptable once those findings go in email.
- Response bodies are size-capped at 3MB (worker concurrency is 5).
- `coreWebVitals.inp` held the `interactive` audit, i.e. Time to Interactive.
  TTI now has its own field; `inp` is left `null` when Lighthouse doesn't
  report it rather than carrying an unrelated metric.
- Tiering rewritten so both axes decide and evidence of ad spend can never be
  skipped, with a new `hot` tier for spend + fixable problems:

  | Prospect | before | after |
  |---|---|---|
  | Established, runs ads, mobile 32/100 | `skip` | **`hot`** |
  | Same, PageSpeed failed | `skip` | `high` + `incomplete_audit` |
  | Genuinely good site, no ad spend | `skip` | `skip` |

- `technically_sound` can no longer coexist with `slow_load`/`no_ssl`, and is
  withheld entirely when the audit is incomplete.

**Note:** tiers are computed at crawl time, so existing prospects keep their
stored tier until re-crawled.

### 2.2 Findings — the personalisation layer

New `backend/src/utils/auditFindings.js`. A catalogue of 13 findings, each
with a stable `code`, a sales `angle`, `severity`, `pitchValue`, an
`applies()` predicate and a `sentence()` built **only** from measured values.
Ranked by `pitchValue × severity`; the top one becomes `topFinding`.

Deliberately deterministic, not LLM-written: every sentence is authored once
by a human with placeholders for measurements, so a claim can never exceed the
data. One verifiably wrong statement about a stranger's website ends that
conversation and, at volume, teaches people to distrust the sender.

Highest-value findings, in rank order: `ads_to_slow_site`, `noindex`,
`no_ssl`, `ads_without_analytics`, `mobile_desktop_gap`, `slow_lcp`,
`no_local_schema`, `no_contact_form`, `missing_meta`, `heavy_page`,
`images_no_alt`, `no_sitemap`.

Actual `buildFindings` output for a prospect with ad tracking and a slow site
(mobile 32, desktop 81, LCP 8.4s, 4.1MB) — generated by running the code, not
written by hand:

```
1. [wasted_ad_spend] Your site has ad conversion tracking installed, but
   Google scores the homepage 32/100 for speed on mobile — any paid traffic
   you send here is largely leaving before the page finishes loading.
2. [mobile_broken]   Your site scores 81/100 on a computer but only 32/100
   on a phone, which is where most of your visitors are.
3. [slow_site]       Your homepage takes 8.4s to show its main content on a
   phone. Google treats anything over 2.5s as a problem.
4. [slow_site]       Your homepage weighs 4.1MB. A typical well-built page is
   under 1MB, so visitors on mobile data pay for the difference in load time.
```

> **Correction (2026-10-15, after independent review).** The earlier version of
> this example was **not reachable from the code**: it listed `mobile_desktop_gap`
> as #2, but `psiPerformanceScoreDesktop` and `pageWeightKb` were read in four
> places and written nowhere — there was no desktop PageSpeed pass and neither
> field existed on the schema, so findings #2 and #4 could never fire and
> `{{perf_desktop}}`/`{{page_weight_mb}}` were permanently empty. Both are now
> actually collected (a gated second PSI call plus Lighthouse's
> `total-byte-weight`), which is what makes the output above real.
>
> The first sentence is also reworded. It previously asserted *"You're running
> paid ads… most of that click spend leaves"* — a claim about the recipient's
> budget inferred from a pixel in their page source. A Meta Pixel outlives the
> campaign that installed it, so that was the highest-ranked finding in the
> catalogue making the claim least supported by the measurement. It now states
> what was actually measured.

Findings are generated at crawl time and stored on the prospect
(`findings[]`, `topFinding`, `topFindingAngle`), so the sentences an email
quotes always derive from the same data that produced the visible score.

Boolean predicates test truthiness rather than `=== false` on purpose: these
objects are Mongoose documents in the worker but plain objects elsewhere, and
a strict comparison silently dropped findings.

### 2.3 The bridge: audit → campaign

**`POST /api/prospect-audits/:id/push-to-campaign`**
Body: `{ campaignId, tiers?, minOpportunity?, requireFindings? }`

- Gated by **both** `prospect_audit` and `campaigns` features — it reads one
  and writes the other, so holding either alone isn't enough.
- Refuses a campaign that isn't `draft` or `paused`; pushing into a live
  campaign would start mailing unreviewed leads within the minute.
- Skips prospects with no email, no findings (unless `requireFindings: false`),
  or already present in the campaign, and reports each count back.
- Each created `CampaignLead` carries an embedded `auditSnapshot`.

**Why embedded rather than a reference to `ProspectAudit`:** sending must not
depend on a join in the worker's hot path, and an audit claim is
point-in-time — if the prospect fixes their site next week, an email already
sent should still reflect what was actually measured. Same write-once
reasoning as `Lead`'s attribution blocks.

UI: a **Push to Campaign** button on the batch page opens a modal to pick the
campaign and tiers, showing live counts of what will be added and what will be
skipped.

### 2.4 Merge tags

`renderMergeTags` in `workers/campaignWorker.js` now resolves, from
`auditSnapshot`:

| Tag | Resolves to |
|---|---|
| `{{top_finding}}` | highest-ranked finding sentence |
| `{{finding_2}}` / `{{finding_3}}` | next ranked sentences |
| `{{findings_list}}` | `<ul>` of all findings |
| `{{perf_score}}` / `{{perf_desktop}}` | mobile / desktop PageSpeed score |
| `{{seo_score}}` | PageSpeed SEO score |
| `{{lcp_seconds}}` | largest-contentful-paint, 1 decimal |
| `{{page_weight_mb}}` | homepage weight in MB |
| `{{website}}` / `{{audit_tier}}` | audited URL / tier |

Two safety properties, both previously absent:

1. **Everything substituted into the BODY is HTML-escaped**, including the
   pre-existing name/email tags — those come from imported CSVs and public
   forms. The **subject** is escaped differently: it is a header, not markup,
   so it gets single-line enforcement (control characters and U+2028/9
   stripped, so a newline in an imported cell cannot terminate the Subject
   header) and **no** HTML escaping.
2. **A campaign can't send a half-rendered email.** `campaignReadiness`
   refuses to start a campaign whose subject/body/follow-up uses audit tags
   unless every pending lead's snapshot can fill **the specific tags that
   template uses** — checked per tag against the field it reads, so a lead
   whose PageSpeed pass failed is caught by `{{perf_score}}` even though it
   has a `topFinding`. It re-runs on `updateCampaign` while a campaign is
   live, so the gate cannot be defeated by editing the body or switching
   `followUpEnabled` on after the start. Tag detection and the tag→field map
   live in `utils/auditMergeTags.js`, and the renderer itself in
   `utils/campaignRender.js`, so both can be required and tested without
   loading the worker (which would spin up a BullMQ Worker as a side effect).

> **Correction (2026-10-15, after independent review).** An earlier version of
> this section claimed the half-rendered-email guard was already complete. It
> was not: it checked only that *a snapshot existed* (`prospectId` set,
> `topFinding` non-empty), which a prospect whose PageSpeed pass failed passes
> — leaving `perfMobile`/`lcpMs`/`pageWeightKb` null and rendering
> "Google scores it /100 on mobile". It also claimed the worker failed any
> lead that slipped through; no such per-lead check existed. Both are fixed
> above, and the false code comment has been removed.

### 2.5 Follow-up: automatic after 2 real opens

The requested change was "2 or more opens → automatic follow-up". Implemented
as `HOT_OPEN_THRESHOLD = 2` (was 3), but counting **qualified** opens:

- New `CampaignLead.humanOpenCount`, incremented only when an open passes the
  scanner/prefetch filter already used to gate the "opened" notification
  (known scanner UA, or a hit within 15s of send). `openCount` still counts
  every pixel hit, so existing analytics are unchanged.
- **Why this matters at a threshold of 2:** a security gateway plus an image
  proxy can easily produce two raw opens for someone who never read the email.
  Following up on that wastes a send and the mailbox's reputation budget. With
  qualified counting, "2 opens" means two plausible human opens.
- **Back-compat:** leads created before this field have `humanOpenCount: 0`
  and would silently stop qualifying, so the original raw-open rule is kept at
  its stricter value (`LEGACY_RAW_OPEN_THRESHOLD = 3`) and OR'd in. Purely
  additive — no in-flight campaign loses its follow-ups.
- The dispatcher now also refuses to queue a follow-up whose subject or body
  is empty. `followUpEnabled` and the follow-up content are separate fields,
  so the flag could be true with nothing to send, and the worker would have
  sent a blank email.

Verified:

```
2 real opens            -> hot     (the requested behaviour)
1 real open             -> not hot
2 scanner-only opens    -> not hot
legacy lead, 3 raw opens-> still hot
legacy lead, 2 raw opens-> not hot
```

**Still required to actually send:** `followUpEnabled` is per-campaign and
defaults to **false**, and a follow-up subject and body must be written. That
default was left alone deliberately — flipping it would start sending from
existing campaigns without anyone choosing to.

### 2.6 Security and correctness (earlier in the session)

31 fixes, committed separately. Summarised in `BACKLOG.md` §0; the headline
items were:

- **Socket room bypass** — any authenticated socket, including a client-portal
  login, could join `admin:syslog` (live server logs), `admin`, or
  `user:<anyoneElse>` just by naming it.
- **Portal isolation** — clients could list all staff and all client records,
  read every staff work log, modify any todo, and DM any employee.
- **Privilege escalation** — a manager could create an admin, and could reset
  an existing admin's password and log in as them.
- **Social OAuth never stored a token** (Mongoose drops virtuals in
  `findOneAndUpdate`), so publishing could never have worked.
- **IMAP reply sync** read a UID range as sequence numbers, fetching only the
  newest message.
- **Members saw an empty app** — an uncaught 403 rejected the whole data load.
- Mass assignment on invoices/services/meetings/projects, upload stored XSS,
  unauthenticated leave attachments, Mongo operator injection, campaign
  tracking XSS/open redirect/scanner-triggered state changes, regex injection.

## 3. The flow, end to end

### 3.1 What a person does

1. **Create a batch and import prospects** — CSV or a public Google Sheet.
   Import is unchanged by any of this work; there is no scraper integration.
2. **Start Crawl.** The batch flips to `crawling` and the dispatcher takes over.
3. **Review the tiers** when it completes. `hot` means evidence of marketing
   spend *and* fixable problems — the strongest pitch in the list.
4. **Get the prospects into a campaign**, either way:
   * **Manually** — the **Push to Campaign** button: choose a draft/paused
     campaign and which tiers to include.
   * **Automatically (optional, OFF by default)** — the **Auto-push** button:
     choose a campaign once, and every future completion of that batch pushes
     itself. Leaving it off changes nothing about the manual flow.
5. **Write the email** in the campaign, using audit merge tags:

   ```
   Hi team at {{first_name}},

   {{top_finding}}

   Happy to send the full breakdown for {{website}} if useful.
   ```

6. **Press Start.** A person always presses Start — nothing in this system
   starts a campaign on its own.

### 3.2 What the machine does

```
CSV / Google Sheet
      │
      ▼
ProspectAuditBatch ──Start Crawl──▶ status: crawling
      │
      ▼  prospectAuditDispatcher  (API process, every minute)
   claims up to 30 prospects/tick, round-robins 2 PageSpeed keys,
   charges 2 PSI calls per prospect against a 25,000/day/key budget
      │
      ▼  prospect-audit-worker  (separate process, concurrency 5)
   Tier 1: fetch homepage + cheerio  → SSL, viewport, title/meta, headings,
           images/alt, JSON-LD (@graph walked), sitemap/robots, analytics,
           ad pixel, CMS, broken-link sample
   Tier 2: PageSpeed mobile (60s budget, 1 retry)
           → scores, LCP/CLS, total-byte-weight → pageWeightKb
   Tier 2b: PageSpeed desktop — ONLY when mobile < 70, since the only thing
           that reads it is the mobile-vs-desktop gap finding
      │
      ▼  prospectScoring + auditFindings  (at crawl time)
   technicalScore (need) × opportunityScore (budget signal) → tier
   13 findings ranked by pitchValue × severity → findings[], topFinding
      │
      ├── manual:    POST /:id/push-to-campaign
      └── automatic: autoCompleteFinishedBatches → autoPushCompletedBatch
                     (atomic claim, authority re-check, fails safe)
      │
      ▼  prospectPushService  (ONE implementation, both callers)
   draft/paused only · dedupes by campaign+email · skips no-email and
   no-findings · embeds a point-in-time auditSnapshot on each CampaignLead
      │
      ▼  campaign stays DRAFT  ◀── a person presses Start
      │
      ▼  checkCampaignReady  — the send-safety gate
      │
      ▼  campaignDispatcher  (API process, every minute)
   one email per campaign per tick, paced by minGap 9min + random 5min,
   daily cap 30, sending window, per-mailbox warm-up limits
      │
      ▼  campaign-worker  (separate process)
   renders merge tags (body HTML-escaped, subject header-safe),
   injects open pixel + tracked links + unsubscribe, sends via SMTP
      │
      ▼  tracking + follow-up
   opens (raw and qualified), clicks, IMAP reply detection, unsubscribe
   → 2 qualified opens or a call request, then 48h → ONE follow-up
```

### 3.3 The safety gates, and what each one stops

| Gate | Stops |
|---|---|
| `checkCampaignReady` on Start/Schedule | Starting a campaign whose email quotes audit facts that some pending lead cannot fill |
| `checkAuditTagData` on campaign **edit** | Adding an audit tag to a **live** campaign its leads cannot fill (and no longer blocks unrelated edits — round 3) |
| `importLeads` guard | Importing unaudited leads into a **live** audit-tag campaign, which would have sent blanks (round 4) |
| `prospectPushService` draft/paused check | Any push — manual or automatic — into a running campaign |
| Auto-push atomic claim | The same batch pushing twice when dispatcher ticks overlap |
| Auto-push authority re-check | Automation continuing on the stale permissions of someone whose access was revoked |
| `followUpStillValid` | A queued follow-up reaching someone who has since replied, unsubscribed or bounced |
| `escHeader` on the subject | HTML escapes in subject lines, and header injection from a newline in an imported CSV cell |
| `decodeEntities` in `stripToPlain` | The plain-text part of an email showing `You&#39;re` |

Every one of these **fails closed**: it refuses to send rather than sending
something wrong. That is the deliberate bias of the whole pipeline, because
the failure mode it exists to prevent is a confident false claim about a
stranger's own website.

### 3.4 Processes that must be running

| Process | Needed for |
|---|---|
| `npm start` (API) | HTTP, sockets, and the campaign/follow-up/reply/audit/ads/billing crons |
| `npm run campaign-worker` | Actually sending campaign and follow-up email |
| `npm run prospect-audit-worker` | Actually crawling and scoring prospect sites |
| `npm run worker` | Transactional email (task/billing/meeting) |
| `npm run social-publish-worker` | Social posting |

MongoDB and Redis are required by all of them.

## 4. Verification

No test runner exists in this repo. Verification was:

- `node --check` on every changed backend file, plus a module smoke-load
  (catches bad requires and undefined references that syntax checks miss).
- `npm run build` for the frontend.
- Behavioural probes against **real MongoDB** (a throwaway database, never the
  dev one) for everything with a query in it, and pure-function probes for the
  rest. Each probe asserts the before/after so it would fail if the change
  were reverted.
- **Independent review.** A second session (Claude B) reviewed every round
  against its own probes rather than rerunning mine, and verified the
  importLeads fix by sweeping *every* code path that can create or revive a
  sendable lead — not just the one that changed.
- **Email rendering proven on the wire**: the real renderer output was sent
  through nodemailer into a MailHog sink and the received message inspected.
  Subjects carry real apostrophes and ampersands, there is exactly one
  `Subject` header, a `Bcc:` smuggled into a business name was neutralised
  into inert text, and the text/plain part is entity-free.

**Still unproven — needs your PageSpeed key.** No real PageSpeed call has been
made (keyless requests return 429), so `total-byte-weight` and the
`strategy=desktop` response shape are confirmed by reasoning only. It fails
closed: if either is wrong, `pageWeightKb`/`perfDesktop` stay null, the
`heavy_page` and `mobile_desktop_gap` findings never fire, and a campaign
using `{{page_weight_mb}}`/`{{perf_desktop}}` refuses to start rather than
sending a blank.

**Also unproven:** no real campaign has been sent; the new UI is
build-verified but no browser has rendered it; the audit cron hook has not run
inside a live dispatcher process.

## 6. Open items

### Known and deliberately left
- **One-line temp-file leak**: a *refused* CSV import leaves its multer temp
  file behind, because the guard returns before the cleanup call. Reviewed as
  non-blocking; offered as a one-line change.

  record** — the stored line is labelled "Last automatic run" instead.

  when mobile scores under 70, so a template using that tag refuses to start
  for every prospect at 70 or above.
